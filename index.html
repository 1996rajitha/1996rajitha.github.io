Research

My MPhil work produced a multi-modal underwater robot — a bio-inspired, multi-arm vehicle whose mechanism switches between propulsion modes without surfacing. I designed the mechanism, simulated the flow around it in ANSYS Fluent with dynamic mesh motion, and validated the predictions in tank experiments.

The question underneath it is a simple one: how does a body change shape to move efficiently through water, and can a single machine carry more than one way of swimming? That sits where mechanism design, hydrodynamics and control meet, and it is where I want to keep working — undulatory and multi-modal propulsion, hydrodynamic characterisation, and the marine energy systems that run on the same physics.

The lab

Every tool below is a single HTML file that runs with no install and no internet, because that is what a Sri Lankan engineering lab can actually rely on. They are open in the browser you are reading this in — click and use them.

In development

NUCLEO-F411RE bench

A Wokwi-style STM32 simulator that mirrors the CubeMX and CubeIDE workflow, so pin configuration feels like the real thing. Students drag in LEDs, resistors, sensors and motors, wire them up, and watch PWM change how a motor responds. It is heading towards a full line-following robot they can build and drive on screen.

In development

PCB Academy

A teaching environment rather than another PCB editor. It walks students through schematic capture, nets, footprints, placement, routing, design rule checks and what the manufacturer actually needs — as transferable skill, so the knowledge carries into KiCad, Altium or Cadence instead of into one tool's buttons. Guided missions, scored.

StudioPad Pro

A digital audio workstation that runs in a browser tab. Real sampled instruments, a vocal recorder with overdub and noise reduction, a song wizard, and a module that turns a hummed melody into notes using a YIN pitch tracker. It ships as an offline package. The signal processing here is the same thinking I use on sensor data.

In use at KDU

Design briefs, marked automatically

Inside KDU-8085 sits the part I am proudest of. Students get a real control problem with a memory map, a specification and a marking scheme. Their whole program is then run against timed sensor scenarios and graded on how it behaves, not on how its source looks — so there is one honest answer to "does it work", and they get it in seconds.
