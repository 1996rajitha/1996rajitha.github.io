Research

MPhil, 2022 to 2025

My MPhil produced a multi-modal underwater robot: an octopus-inspired, multi-arm vehicle whose mechanism switches between propulsion modes without surfacing. I designed the mechanism, simulated the flow around it in ANSYS Fluent with dynamic mesh motion, and validated the predictions in tank experiments.

The question underneath it is a simple one: how does a body change shape to move efficiently through water, and can a single machine carry more than one way of swimming? That sits where mechanism design, hydrodynamics and control meet, and it is where I want to keep working: undulatory and multi-modal propulsion, hydrodynamic characterisation, and the marine energy systems that run on the same physics.

Doctoral direction

Proposed, for a 2027 start

Kelp forests and fish schools both reshape the flow around each of their members. My doctoral question is whether engineered energy harvesters, arranged as a colony, can work with that interaction instead of against it.

Adaptation
How can kelp-like compliant structures reconfigure under waves and currents, so they keep converting energy across sea states and ride out the extreme ones?
Hybrid surfaces
What do fish-scale-inspired elements add when they are built into those structures?
The colony
Does a colony of harvesters, spaced and phased with intent, capture more than the same devices working apart, and which arrangements matter most?
Coexistence
How can such a colony share the water with the ecosystem around it?

The methods carry over from my MPhil: mechanism design, CFD with moving boundaries extended to fluid–structure interaction for compliant bodies, and scaled tank experiments to validate the simulations. Alongside, I am writing a critical review of bio-inspired adaptive systems for marine energy harvesting, conversion and environmental coexistence, to map where this idea sits in the literature.

If this overlaps with your group’s work, I would be glad to talk.

In use at KDU

KDU-8085

An 8085 simulator that grew from a simple lab into the platform my microprocessor module is built on. Students write assembly, watch it move through the datapath, and solve design briefs marked on how their program behaves.

  • Two-pass assembler with micro-step datapath animation
  • 8255A PPI, I/O and pin-level benches, a stack visualiser and a bit lab
  • A bytes and cycles bench for T-states, machine cycles and addressing modes
  • Design briefs graded automatically against timed sensor scenarios

NUCLEO-F411RE bench

A Wokwi-style simulator for the NUCLEO-F411RE, built the same single-file, offline way as KDU-8085. It mirrors the STM32CubeMX and CubeIDE workflow, so pin set-up and code feel like the real board.

  • Pin and peripheral set-up in the CubeMX style
  • Drag-and-drop parts: LEDs, resistors, sensors, motors and drivers
